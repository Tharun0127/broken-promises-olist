The Promise Gap

Do late deliveries cause bad reviews and lost repeat purchases on the Olist marketplace, and what should Olist test to fix it? Built from the public Olist data (orders from 2016-09-04 to 2018-08-29).

AnswerPartly. Late orders get far more 1-star reviews (53.8% vs 6.6% on time), but missing the promise by one day is not the main cause. Once the trend is allowed for, the jump at the promise date is -1.7 pp (95% CI -4.9 pp to +1.5 pp). The damage builds over the following days: 13.0% 1-star on day 1, 42.5% on day 3, the point where most late customers are surveyed before the parcel arrives. There is no detectable effect on repeat purchase (+0.1 pp, CI -2.0 pp to +2.2 pp), and repeat purchase is so rare (2.0% within 180 days) that even the worst case is R$ 2,554 of order value a quarter.
RecommendationTest a proactive delay message for orders that miss their date, with survey timing held the same in both arms. It needs 1,245 late orders per arm, about 5.7 months at 2017 to 2018 volumes. Separately, stop sending the review survey before the parcel arrives.

This is a design, not a run experiment.

  • Hypothesis: a proactive message with a revised date, sent the day after a missed promise, changes the 1-star rate of late orders.
  • Unit: customer (customer_unique_id), 50/50. Low interference: one customer's message does not change another's delivery. Seller-level randomisation would cut the number of units for no gain.
  • Primary metric: 1-star rate of flagged late orders (baseline 56.0%).
  • Guardrails: review response rate (97.9% today), cancellations, service contacts, delivery time.
  • Not primary: repeat purchase. It would need 104,314 first orders per arm.
  • Pitfalls handled: fixed sample size (no peeking), daily sample ratio mismatch check, novelty check across halves, analysis limited to flagged orders.
